The end is a pipeline that buys, improves, and sells companies — ideally while the [[Restore Marketing Co]] and [[HP Landscaping]] cash flows underwrite the lifestyle. Every operating decision made today should be asked: does this move the owner closer to M&A capable, or keep them stuck in the weeds?

## Target Criteria

> TODO: Define with the user. These are Claude's placeholder defaults based on the Q2 focus (agent-orchestration-first operator) — revise as the user's real criteria crystallize.

- **Size**: small enough to buy solo, big enough to matter ($250K–$3M revenue range?)
- **Owner dependency**: high (because the buyer's edge is replacing the owner with agents)
- **Systems maturity**: low (again, because the edge is bringing in automation the seller couldn't build)
- **Industry**: adjacent to existing operator experience (marketing + home services?) — but open to anything the M&A math works on
- **Deal structure**: seller financing / earn-out friendly (to reduce cash-out-of-pocket)
- **Exit path**: clear 3–5 year exit route (strategic buyer, PE roll-up, owner-operator handoff)

## Exit Readiness — Current Companies

What has to be true before [[Restore Marketing Co]] or [[HP Landscaping]] can be sold?

- [ ] Books clean and reviewable (3 years of P&L minimum)
- [ ] Standard Operating Procedures documented (every recurring process)
- [ ] Owner replaceable (agents + employees handle day-to-day; owner is not the bottleneck)
- [ ] Customer concentration <20% (no single customer >20% of revenue)
- [ ] Revenue growing or at least stable year-over-year
- [ ] Legal clean: contracts in writing, IP owned by the LLC, no verbal handshake deals

Each of these becomes a work note when actively being built out.
